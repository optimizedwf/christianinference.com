Technology should
answer to people.

Catholic Computer explores sovereign computing, decentralized intelligence, and open-source AI, grounded in human dignity and Catholic social teaching.

People should be able to keep their lives private, own their data, choose their tools, and exercise their judgment without covert algorithmic manipulation. We want to help make that possible.

Our starting principles

The person comes first.

Christian ethics should shape how technology works, not just the answers it gives. These are the standards we want to translate into design decisions, public research, and working software.

Privacy and ownership

Keep sensitive information under the user's control. Favor local processing, data minimization, inspectable data flows, and the ability to export or delete what a system holds.

Free will and conscience

No covert persuasion, engineered dependency, or exploitation of personal vulnerabilities. People must remain free to question, disagree, decide, and disconnect.

Decentralized intelligence

Explore open models, portable agents, interoperable tools, and computing infrastructure that individuals and communities can run or choose for themselves.

Truth and accountability

Make claims traceable to sources and technical results reproducible. Be clear about uncertainty. Keep people responsible for consequential decisions.

Subsidiarity and the common good

Support the autonomy of families, parishes, schools, and local communities. Share knowledge and tools so advanced computing is useful beyond large institutions.

Stewardship

Use compute responsibly. Favor efficient models, durable hardware, repairability, and transparent resource costs when choosing how to build.
